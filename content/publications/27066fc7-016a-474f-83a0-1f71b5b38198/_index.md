  description: How individual differences in brain network organization track behavioral
    variability is a fundamental question in systems neuroscience. Recent work suggests
    that resting-state and task-state functional connectivity can predict specific traits
    at the individual level. However, most studies focus on single behavioral traits,
    thus not capturing broader relationships across behaviors. In a large sample of
    1858 typically developing children from the Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development
    (ABCD) study, we show that predictive network features are distinct across the domains
    of cognitive performance, personality scores and mental health assessments. On the
    other hand, traits within each behavioral domain are predicted by similar network
    features. Predictive network features and models generalize to other behavioral
    measures within the same behavioral domain. Although tasks are known to modulate
    the functional connectome, predictive network features are similar between resting
    and task states. Overall, our findings reveal shared brain network features that
    account for individual variation within broad domains of behavior in childhood.
