  description: "This paper examines whether biological age, as distinct from chronological\
    \ age, could become a new basis for discrimination. Drawing on Joona R\xE4s\xE4\
    nen\u2019s proposal that individuals be allowed to change their legal age to mitigate\
    \ chronological age-based discrimination, it assesses whether this could introduce\
    \ discrimination based on biological age. The analysis distinguishes chronological\
    \ from biological age and clarifies differences. Chronological age measures the\
    \ time elapsed since an entity\u2019s existence, with unidirectional and perfect\
    \ passage-of-time correlation. Biological age is the attempt to quantify the state\
    \ of the ageing process\u2014gradual deterioration of body and mind\u2014via biomarkers\
    \ compared to population averages. It is variable and potentially reversible. For\
    \ these reasons, biological age currently isn\u2019t familiar to the public, it\
    \ lacks stability and institutional recognition, and thus does not meet the conditions\
    \ for discrimination as discussed by prominent theories of discrimination by Lippert-Rasmussen\
    \ and Behrendt. Therefore, individuals are unlikely to face discrimination on this\
    \ basis in the same sense as they might based on chronological age. However, if\
    \ biological age were formally adopted \u2013 say, through legal reforms substituting\
    \ or even replacing chronological age \u2013 it could acquire normative significance\
    \ and potentially serve as a new basis for discrimination. Hence, openly available\
    \ information about biological age should be limited to mitigate the risk that it\
    \ could become a basis for discrimination."
