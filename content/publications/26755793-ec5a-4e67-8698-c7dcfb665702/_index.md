    beneficial and should always be deeply integrated into cognitive systems. Instead,
    it proposes a taxonomy that recognizes the existence of hostile and detrimental
    tools and suggests that some tools might be better suited remaining on the periphery
    of an extended cognitive system. The chapter explores the moral implications of
    deep integration between cognitive tools and systems, highlighting three potential
    detriments: narrows, detours, and dead ends. These adverse effects present moral
    challenges and call for cautious consideration during technology development. The
    text emphasizes that the list of detrimental effects is likely to expand as technology
