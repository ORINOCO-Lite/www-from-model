  description: Sex differences in the brain are widely studied, but results are often
    inconsistent and it is assumed that many negative findings are not even being reported.
    The lack of consistent findings might be based on the highly questionable assumption
    of a clear-cut sexual dimorphism in brain structure and function, that underlies
    commonly used group comparisons between males and females. Without having to rely
    on this assumption, state of the art statistical learning methods based on large
    neuroimaging data sets might offer the tools necessary to disentangle the complex
    pattern of sex-related variations in brain structure and organization.
