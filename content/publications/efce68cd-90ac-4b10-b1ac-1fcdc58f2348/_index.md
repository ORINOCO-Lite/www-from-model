  description: 'High co-morbidity and substantial overlap across psychiatric disorders
    encourage a transition in psychiatry research from categorical to dimensional approaches
    that integrate neuroscience and psychopathology. Cerebellum is involved in a wide
    range of nonmotor cognitive functions and mental disorders. An important question
    thus centers on the extent to which cerebellar function can be linked to transdiagnostic
    dimensions of psychopathology. Here, this question is investigated using partial
    least squares to identify latent dimensions linking cerebellar connectome properties
    as assessed by macroscale spatial gradients of connectivity to a large set of clinical
    and behavioral measures in 198 participants across diagnostic categories. This analysis
    reveals significant correlated patterns of cerebellar connectivity gradients and
    behavioral measures that could be represented into four latent dimensions: general
    psychopathology, general lack of attention regulation, internalizing symptoms, and
    dysfunctional memory. Each dimension is associated with a distinct spatial pattern
    of cerebellar connectivity gradients. These findings highlight the relevance of
    cerebellar connectivity as a necessity for the study and classification of transdiagnostic
    dimensions of psychopathology.'
