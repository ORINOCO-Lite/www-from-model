  description: 'Numerous neuroimaging studies have investigated the neural mechanisms
    of two mutually independent yet closely related cognitive processes aiding humans
    to navigate complex societies: social hierarchy-related learning (SH-RL) and social
    hierarchy-related interaction (SH-RI). To integrate these heterogeneous results
    into a more fine-grained and reliable characterization of the neural basis of social
    hierarchy, we combined coordinate-based meta-analyses with connectivity and functional
    decoding analyses to understand the underlying neuropsychological mechanism of SH-RL
    and SH-RI. We identified the anterior insula and temporoparietal junction (dominance
    detection), medial prefrontal cortex (information updating and computation), and
    intraparietal sulcus region, amygdala, and hippocampus (social hierarchy representation)
    as consistent activated brain regions for SH-RL, but the striatum, amygdala, and
    hippocampus associated with reward processing for SH-RI. Our results provide an
    overview of the neural architecture of the neuropsychological processes underlying
    how we understand, and interact within, social hierarchy.'
