  description: "Objectives: The adverse health effects of loneliness are well documented,\
    \ but less is known about cultural moderators of this relationship. Contributing\
    \ to the literature, we examined whether cross-cultural differences in individualism\
    \ moderate the effect of loneliness on health. Methods: We used population-based\
    \ longitudinal data of 14 countries (N = 40,797), as provided by the Survey of Health,\
    \ Ageing, and Retirement in Europe data. Multilevel regression analyses were employed.\
    \ Moderating effects were analyzed for multiple health outcomes: activities of daily\
    \ living, instrumental activities of daily living, grip strength, life satisfaction,\
    \ depression, memory performance, verbal fluency, and numeracy. Results: Cultural\
    \ individualism significantly moderated the effect of loneliness on health regarding\
    \ most health outcomes. In general, the effect of loneliness on health became stronger\
    \ in less individualistic/more collectivistic countries. Discussion: Cultural individualism\
    \ proved to be one important moderator of the loneliness\u2013health relationship.\
    \ As previous studies mostly used samples from highly individualistic countries,\
    \ the current literature might severely underestimate the global public health burden\
    \ of loneliness."
