  description: When actions are mediated by means of a brain-computer interface, it
    seems that we cannot assess whether the user is culpable for the action without
    determining whether the brain-computer interface correctly decoded the intentions
    of the user. Here I argue that this requirement is confused. I also argue that,
    at least for the purposes of assessing moral culpability, BCI-mediated action should
    be viewed on the model of action mediated by ordinary (albeit complex) tools.
